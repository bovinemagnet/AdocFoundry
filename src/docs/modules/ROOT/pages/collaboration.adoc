= Working together
Paul Snow
:revnumber: 0.0.0
:description: Share a workspace from your computer and let others edit its pages with you, or join someone else's session.

AdocFoundry lets two or more people edit the same AsciiDoc pages at the same time.
One person shares a workspace, and the others join it from their own copy of the app.
This page describes both sides.

There is no account to make and nothing is stored in the cloud.
The host's app is the server: it listens for connections, decides who comes in and holds the files.
Guests see and edit the host's pages, and the host's computer is the only one that saves them.

Collaboration is off until you set `[collaboration] enabled = true` in your user settings file.
Until then nothing about it appears in the menus or the command palette, and nothing listens or connects.
A workspace settings file cannot turn it on.
See xref:settings.adoc[Settings].

Every action here is a command, so it also appears in the command palette and in the *File* menu.

== Sharing a workspace

Choose *Share Workspace…* from the *File* menu or the command palette.
A dialog shows what would be shared before anything happens.

What is offered:: The `.adoc` pages and partials under the first folder of the workspace.
The dialog counts them, and says how many AsciiDoc files are left out.
Files are left out when they are in a hidden folder or an ignored folder (see `workspace.ignore` in xref:settings.adoc[Settings]), lead outside the folder through a link, or are too large.

What is never offered:: Images, settings, the `.git` folder and any other file that is not an AsciiDoc page or partial.

Where it listens:: You choose.
*This computer only* is for trying it out, or for people using the same computer.
*Local network* shows your address on the local network, for people on the same network or a VPN.
Nothing listens until you press one of these buttons, and each person you invite still waits for you to admit them.

After you choose, the *Session* panel opens in the sidebar.
It shows the folder shared, the address listened on, the safety code, who is waiting, who is in and the shared files that are open.

NOTE: You can share one workspace at a time, and you cannot share while you are in someone else's session.

=== Inviting someone

Press *Copy Invitation* in the *Session* panel, or choose it from the *File* menu.
The invitation is one line of text, which begins `adocfoundry:join`, placed on your clipboard.

* It can be used once, by one person.
* It lasts ten minutes. The panel says how long the last one has left.
* It is never saved. Copy another whenever you need one.

Send it to the person by a channel you trust, such as a direct message or a conversation in person.
Anyone who has the invitation within those ten minutes can knock at the door, so do not post it somewhere others can read it.

=== The safety code

The *Session* panel shows a safety code for your session, written as four groups of five digits.
It identifies your session's certificate.
When someone joins, their app shows a code too, and the two must be the same.
Compare them by voice or in person, not by the channel that carried the invitation.
If the codes differ, the guest should not go on.

=== Letting people in

When someone has connected and compared the code, they appear under *Waiting* in the *Session* panel as "asks to join".
You choose:

*Admit*:: They join as a Viewer.
*Turn Away*:: They are refused.

A waiting request that you do not answer is turned away after ten minutes.

Each participant under *Participants* has a role:

Viewer:: May read and copy, and cannot edit.
Editor:: May edit the shared pages.

Use *Let Edit* to make a Viewer an Editor, and *Make Viewer* to take editing away again.
Taking editing away applies at once, to everything that person sends from then on.
*Remove* sends someone out of the session; a removed person cannot resume.

=== While you are sharing

A file a guest opens does not appear in your tabs.
It stays open in the background.
Choose *Show*, beside a shared file in the *Session* panel, to open it in a tab.
Closing a shared tab hides it again.
It does not close the file for your guests.

Commands that change more than one file, or the structure of the folder, are refused while you share:

* renaming, moving or deleting files;
* New Page, New Module and New Project;
* adding a page to navigation;
* Replace across the workspace;
* pasting or dropping images;
* opening another folder.

Stop sharing to use them.

While you share, diagrams drawn by other programs (Graphviz, PlantUML and D2) are not drawn on any page, since a page may include a partial a guest has written.
Diagrams already drawn before are still shown, and Mermaid diagrams are drawn as usual.

Guests are never told where your files are on your computer.
The messages they see name no paths.

On a guest's side, renaming or deleting a shared page, its local history and every export are refused.

=== Stopping

*Stop Sharing* in the *Session* panel or the *File* menu closes every connection and stops listening.
Your pages stay open as ordinary pages of your workspace.
A background page with unsaved changes opens as a tab, so that you can save it.
The other background pages close.

== Joining a session

Choose *Join Session…* from the *File* menu or the command palette.

. Paste the invitation you were sent, check your name (it starts as your account's name, and the host sees it), and choose *Connect*.
Under your name, the optional *E-mail for commit credit (optional)* field is how the host can credit you in Git, as xref:collaboration.adoc#_credit_for_guests[Credit for guests] describes.
. The app connects and checks that the host holds the certificate the invitation names.
If it does not, nothing is sent and the app says so.
. A dialog shows the safety code.
Compare it with the code in the host's *Session* panel, and choose *Join* only if they are the same.
Your name and the invitation's secret are sent only after you choose.
. The *Session* panel says "Waiting to be let in…" until the host admits you.
You join as a Viewer, until the host lets you edit.

=== Opening shared files

Once you are in, the *Session* panel lists the shared files under *Shared files*, partials marked as such.
Choose *Open* beside a file to open it in a tab.
It opens once the host's text arrives.

A shared tab is marked with "(shared)" after its name.
It is the host's file, not one on your computer:

* it has no file on your disk and is never saved there;
* *Save* asks the host to save it, which works only if you are an Editor;
* if you are a Viewer, or the connection is down, the page is read only.

The *Session* panel says whether you may edit, and lists everyone in the session, with "(you)" beside yourself.

Links and includes in a shared page lead only to other files the host shares.
Antora resource ids, such as `+xref:other.adoc[]+` and `+include::partial$note.adoc[]+`, resolve among the shared files.
They use the host's component names and versions.
Your own copy of the same project is never mixed in, even if it is open in the same window.

An include shows in the preview once the included shared file is open.
Until then it shows as unresolved.
Open the partial from the *Session* panel.

Following a link to another shared file asks the host for it.
Use Go to Definition, or click the link in the preview.
The file opens in a tab when it arrives.

Some things are not shared:

* Pictures are not shared. The preview shows a picture's alternative text instead.
* Diagrams drawn by other programs (Graphviz, PlantUML and D2) are not drawn for a shared page. Mermaid diagrams are.
* The host shares only the names and versions of its Antora components, never their attributes or navigation. Attributes set in `antora.yml` are not known on your side.
* Shared pages do not appear in search, Quick Open, recent files or the link graph.

== What the status bar says

While a shared page is active, the status bar shows who it is shared by, what you may do and whether it is saved, for example "Shared · Editor · Saved".

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Shows |Meaning

|Shared by you
|You are the host.

|Shared · Editor
|You joined, and may edit.

|Shared · read only
|You joined as a Viewer, or you may not edit just now.

|Shared · disconnected
|You may not edit and the connection to the host is down.
|===

The second part is the save state:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Shows |Meaning

|Saved
|The host's file on disk holds this text.

|Unsaved changes
|The shared text differs from the host's file.

|Changed on disk
|The host's file was changed outside the session, and the host must decide which to keep.

|Not known saved
|Your connection is down, so you cannot tell.
|===

== When the connection drops

If a guest's connection drops, a notification says so, and the *Session* panel shows "Disconnected: not known saved".
You cannot edit until the connection is back.
Choose *Reconnect* in the panel to connect again; the host keeps your place until it removes you or stops sharing.
When you are back, the app brings your pages up to date with the host's.

*Leave Session* closes your connection for good.
Your shared tabs stay open, but they are no longer shared and are not saved on your computer.

If the host stops sharing, the session ends in the same way.

Edits you made just before the connection dropped, which had not yet reached the host, are sent again when you reconnect.
Nothing you typed is lost by a short drop.

There is one exception.
If the host reloaded the page from disk, or started it again, while you were away, you start again from the host's new text.
Edits of yours that had not reached the host are gone.
The *Session* panel and the status bar show the host's text and save state as they are.

The host having your edit is not the same as it being saved.
"Saved" in the status bar means the host's file on disk holds the text.
Until the host saves, edits are only in the shared text.

== If something goes wrong

The host's file on disk is the reference.
A guest's tabs are never saved on the guest's computer.

If a page looks wrong on a guest's side, for example it does not match what the host sees:

. Copy anything you want to keep.
. Choose *Leave Session*.
. Join again with a new invitation.

Your pages start again from the host's text.

If the host's page looks wrong, the host can save it, stop sharing and check it as usual.
After *Stop Sharing*, pages with unsaved changes stay open in tabs so they can be saved.
Local history keeps earlier saves.

If a file was changed on disk during a session, the status bar says "Changed on disk", and the host chooses which text to keep.
See <<_what_the_status_bar_says,what the status bar says>>.

== Firewalls

When you share on the local network, your operating system may ask whether to allow AdocFoundry to accept incoming connections.
Allow it for the network you are on, or guests cannot connect.
Sharing on *This computer only* never needs this.

The host listens on a port the operating system chooses each time you share.
The invitation names it.
Nothing listens while you are not sharing.

On macOS, the first time you share on the local network, or join someone on it, macOS may ask whether AdocFoundry may find devices on your local network.
Allow it, or the connection cannot be made.

== Seeing each other

In a shared page, the source editor shows where the other participants are.
Each person has a colour, the same in every view.

* A selection is filled in the person's colour.
* Their caret is a frame round the character it is on.
* A mark in the gutter, in their colour, shows the line their caret is on.

Colour is never the only sign of who is who.
The *Session* panel names each participant, shows their colour and says where their caret is, for example "page.adoc, line 12".

Carets follow the text.
When anyone edits, the carets and selections after the edit move with the words they were on, so they stay in the right place.
Your own caret and selection are sent to the others at most every 50 milliseconds, the latest one winning, so typing and moving about are never held up.
When someone leaves, or their connection drops, their caret and selection disappear.

If you are using an input method to compose text, such as Japanese or Chinese, or accents, your composition carries on when someone else edits elsewhere in the page.
It is marked again where their edit moved it.

== Following someone

You can make your view follow another participant.
It then goes where they go.

=== Starting

In the *Session* panel, each other participant who is connected has a *Follow* button.
Choose it, and your view follows them.

* When they move to another shared page, that page opens on your side and becomes your active page.
* While they type and move, their caret stays in view.
Your editor scrolls only when their caret would leave it.
* If they are on a page that is not open on your side, it is fetched from the host and shown when it arrives.
That happens for a guest.
A host has every shared page already.

The status bar says "Following" and their name.
The button beside them in the *Session* panel then reads *Stop Following*.

=== Following a follower

If you follow someone who is following someone else, you go to whoever they follow.
If people follow each other in a ring, nobody moves.

=== Where people are

The *Session* panel says which shared page each person is on, even when it is not open on your side.
Everyone in a session can see which shared page each other person is on.
A page that is not shared is never named.
Someone on a page of their own shows as on no shared page.

=== When following stops

Following stops when you:

* type or undo;
* click or scroll in the editor;
* choose another tab or page yourself;
* choose *Stop Following*, beside them in the *Session* panel or in the *File* menu.

Following also stops when the person leaves the session.
If they disconnect, or move to a page that is not shared, your view stays where it is.

Following does not send anything about your own caret beyond what is already shared.
It does not change what you may edit.

== Comments

Everyone with a shared page open sees its comments, on the same text whoever types, and Viewers can comment and reply too.
The host keeps them in its comments file beside the page.
Suggested changes work the same way.
A Viewer may suggest but not accept, while an Editor and the host may accept, and the host applies each accept once.
See xref:comments.adoc#_suggesting_a_change[Suggesting a change] and xref:comments.adoc[Comments].

== Credit for guests

In a session only the host writes files and commits.
Without anything more, every commit would carry everyone's edits under the host's name.
Git has a convention for crediting others: a `Co-authored-by: Name <email>` line at the end of the commit message.
GitHub, GitLab and other services show these lines beside the commit.
AdocFoundry uses it to credit the guests who changed the files a commit takes.
A guest whose suggestion is accepted is credited for that page as if it had typed the change, whoever accepts it, the guest itself included.
As always, the guest needs a usable address.

=== Giving your address

*Join Session…* has a field, *E-mail for commit credit (optional)*, under your name.
It is filled in from `[comments] email` in your user settings file, or else from your global Git `user.email`.
You can change it or empty it.

If what you type is not an e-mail address, "Not an e-mail address" shows under the field, and *Connect* waits until you fix it or empty it.
Without an address you are not credited.

Only the host is told your address.
Other guests never see it, and it is not shown in the *Session* panel.
The host also keeps your comments on a shared page with your address, so the page's comments file, which is usually committed, holds it, as it does for the host's own comments.
Because the host knows your comments by your address, you can edit your own comments in a later session, even under another name.

=== Crediting guests when you commit

When you are the host and commit, a *Co-authors* list appears under the files in the commit dialog if guests changed any of the files the commit takes.
That means the files you gave and anything already staged.
There is one checkbox for each guest, ticked, with their name and address.
Untick anyone who should not be credited.
The commit adds a `Co-authored-by:` line for each guest left ticked.

Nothing shows when nobody else changed those files.
You are never listed, nor is anyone whose address is the commit's author's or committer's.

CAUTION: Nobody checks the address a guest gives, so look at the list before you commit.

=== What counts

A guest is credited for a file only if they changed its text, by typing or deleting.
Opening a file, reading it or moving the caret does not count.

Credit is kept until the files are committed.
It survives the connection dropping and coming back, the page being reloaded from disk, the session ending and the app quitting, because the host's app keeps it in its own state folder.
Renaming a page in the app keeps its credit.
If a file's text goes back to what was last committed, its credit is dropped when the commit dialog opens.

After a commit, the files it took start again with nobody credited.
The exception is a guest who changed a file after it was staged, who is offered again next time.

== Sharing a session or sharing a repository

There are two ways for people to work on the same documentation.
A live session, which this page describes, has everyone edit one copy of the files.
Sharing a repository has everyone edit their own copy and exchange work through Git.
They suit different moments, and it helps to know which one you are in.

=== A live session

Everyone edits the host's own working copy, as the host's app holds it.

* Only the host's files change, and only the host saves.
* A guest's shared tabs are in memory only.
They are never written to the guest's disk, not even as crash recovery, and a guest's *Save* asks the host.
* A guest who also has a clone of the same repository finds it untouched by the session.
* Only the host commits, so a change made in the session is committed once, from one place.
* The host's commits carry everyone's edits under the host's name.
They also credit guests with `Co-authored-by:` lines, chosen in the commit dialog.
See xref:collaboration.adoc#_credit_for_guests[Credit for guests].

If Git or another tool changes a shared file on the host's disk, for example in a checkout or a pull, it becomes a conflict for the host to resolve, and the file is never silently overwritten.
The host keeps the shared text or reloads the file's.
See "Changed on disk" under <<_what_the_status_bar_says,what the status bar says>>.

=== Each person's own clone

Everyone edits their own copy of the repository and shares work through Git: commits, pushes, pulls and merges.
You do not see each other's edits as they are typed.
Each person's commits are their own, and conflicts are resolved by merging.

=== Comparing them

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
| |A live session |Each person's own clone

|Who writes files
|Only the host
|Each person, in their own copy

|Who commits
|Only the host
|Each person

|When others see changes
|Live, as they are typed
|After the work is pushed and pulled

|Authorship
|The host's name on every commit, crediting guests who give an address as co-authors the host chooses
|Each person's own commits

|Conflicts
|A file changed on the host's disk is a conflict for the host to resolve
|Resolved by merging in Git
|===

=== Using both

* Do not edit the same files in your own clone during a session and in the session at once.
The app cannot see edits made outside it, so both versions could end up committed and conflicting.
* When a session ends, the host commits, and pushes if the work is to be shared, what was shared.
The others then pull.

== What is not there yet

* Other participants' carets and selections are drawn in the source editor only, not yet in the visual editor.
* Images and other media are not shared, and guests have no navigation (the trees of navigation files) for shared components.
* There is no relay server, so sharing works across a local network or a VPN, not across the internet through a network you do not control.
