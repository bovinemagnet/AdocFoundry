= Working together
Paul Snow
:revnumber: 0.0.0
:description: Share a workspace from your computer and let others edit its pages with you, or join someone else's session.

AdocFoundry lets two or more people edit the same AsciiDoc pages at the same time.
One person shares a workspace, and the others join it from their own copy of the app.
This page describes both sides.

There is no account to make and nothing is stored in the cloud.
The host's app is the server: it listens for connections, decides who comes in and holds the files.
Guests see and edit the host's pages, and the host's computer is the only one that saves them.

Collaboration is off until you set `[collaboration] enabled = true` in your user settings file.
Until then nothing about it appears in the menus or the command palette, and nothing listens or connects.
A workspace settings file cannot turn it on.
See xref:settings.adoc[Settings].

Every action here is a command, so it also appears in the command palette and in the *File* menu.

== Sharing a workspace

Choose *Share Workspace…* from the *File* menu or the command palette.
A dialog shows what would be shared before anything happens.

What is offered:: The `.adoc` pages and partials under the first folder of the workspace.
The dialog counts them, and says how many AsciiDoc files are left out.
Files are left out when they are in a hidden folder or an ignored folder (see `workspace.ignore` in xref:settings.adoc[Settings]), lead outside the folder through a link, or are too large.

What is never offered:: Images, settings, the `.git` folder and any other file that is not an AsciiDoc page or partial.

Where it listens:: You choose.
*This computer only* is for trying it out, or for people using the same computer.
*Local network* shows your address on the local network, for people on the same network or a VPN.
Nothing listens until you press one of these buttons, and each person you invite still waits for you to admit them.

After you choose, the *Session* panel opens in the sidebar.
It shows the folder shared, the address listened on, the safety code, who is waiting, who is in and the shared files that are open.

NOTE: You can share one workspace at a time, and you cannot share while you are in someone else's session.

=== Inviting someone

Press *Copy Invitation* in the *Session* panel, or choose it from the *File* menu.
The invitation is one line of text, which begins `adocfoundry:join`, placed on your clipboard.

* It can be used once, by one person.
* It lasts ten minutes. The panel says how long the last one has left.
* It is never saved. Copy another whenever you need one.

Send it to the person by a channel you trust, such as a direct message or a conversation in person.
Anyone who has the invitation within those ten minutes can knock at the door, so do not post it somewhere others can read it.

=== The safety code

The *Session* panel shows a safety code for your session, written as four groups of five digits.
It identifies your session's certificate.
When someone joins, their app shows a code too, and the two must be the same.
Compare them by voice or in person, not by the channel that carried the invitation.
If the codes differ, the guest should not go on.

=== Letting people in

When someone has connected and compared the code, they appear under *Waiting* in the *Session* panel as "asks to join".
You choose:

*Admit*:: They join as a Viewer.
*Turn Away*:: They are refused.

A waiting request that you do not answer is turned away after ten minutes.

Each participant under *Participants* has a role:

Viewer:: May read and copy, and cannot edit.
Editor:: May edit the shared pages.

Use *Let Edit* to make a Viewer an Editor, and *Make Viewer* to take editing away again.
Taking editing away applies at once, to everything that person sends from then on.
*Remove* sends someone out of the session; a removed person cannot resume.

=== Stopping

*Stop Sharing* in the *Session* panel or the *File* menu closes every connection and stops listening.
Your pages stay open as ordinary pages of your workspace.

== Joining a session

Choose *Join Session…* from the *File* menu or the command palette.

. Paste the invitation you were sent, check your name (it starts as your account's name, and the host sees it), and choose *Connect*.
. The app connects and checks that the host holds the certificate the invitation names.
If it does not, nothing is sent and the app says so.
. A dialog shows the safety code.
Compare it with the code in the host's *Session* panel, and choose *Join* only if they are the same.
Your name and the invitation's secret are sent only after you choose.
. The *Session* panel says "Waiting to be let in…" until the host admits you.
You join as a Viewer, until the host lets you edit.

=== Opening shared files

Once you are in, the *Session* panel lists the shared files under *Shared files*, partials marked as such.
Choose *Open* beside a file to open it in a tab.
It opens once the host's text arrives.

A shared tab is marked with "(shared)" after its name.
It is the host's file, not one on your computer:

* it has no file on your disk and is never saved there;
* *Save* asks the host to save it, which works only if you are an Editor;
* if you are a Viewer, or the connection is down, the page is read only.

The *Session* panel says whether you may edit, and lists everyone in the session, with "(you)" beside yourself.

== What the status bar says

While a shared page is active, the status bar shows who it is shared by, what you may do and whether it is saved, for example "Shared · Editor · Saved".

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Shows |Meaning

|Shared by you
|You are the host.

|Shared · Editor
|You joined, and may edit.

|Shared · read only
|You joined as a Viewer, or you may not edit just now.

|Shared · disconnected
|You may not edit and the connection to the host is down.
|===

The second part is the save state:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Shows |Meaning

|Saved
|The host's file on disk holds this text.

|Unsaved changes
|The shared text differs from the host's file.

|Changed on disk
|The host's file was changed outside the session, and the host must decide which to keep.

|Not known saved
|Your connection is down, so you cannot tell.
|===

== When the connection drops

If a guest's connection drops, a notification says so, and the *Session* panel shows "Disconnected: not known saved".
You cannot edit until the connection is back.
Choose *Reconnect* in the panel to connect again; the host keeps your place until it removes you or stops sharing.
When you are back, the app brings your pages up to date with the host's.

*Leave Session* closes your connection for good.
Your shared tabs stay open, but they are no longer shared and are not saved on your computer.

If the host stops sharing, the session ends in the same way.

== Firewalls

When you share on the local network, your operating system may ask whether to allow AdocFoundry to accept incoming connections.
Allow it for the network you are on, or guests cannot connect.
Sharing on *This computer only* never needs this.

== What is not there yet

* Other participants' carets and selections are not drawn. They are planned for the next part of the collaboration work.
* Images and other media are not shared. A guest sees only the pages and partials the host offers.
* There is no relay server, so sharing works across a local network or a VPN, not across the internet through a network you do not control.
